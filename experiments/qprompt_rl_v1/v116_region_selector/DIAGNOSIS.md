# V116 post-hoc mechanism diagnosis

Requested by the user after the completed negative pilot. This analysis changes no experiment settings, checkpoints, selection policy or original result. It does not launch a new experiment. The additional operation is CPU scoring of final RL/CE actors on all saved same-seed training decision states: 512 actor forward calls, 0 segmentation forwards, 0 image reads, 0 optimizer updates and 0 new query calls. It is a post-hoc training-state diagnostic, not an independent or deployment-state evaluation. See policy_audit.py and POLICY_AUDIT.json.

## Directly supported findings

1. The final selection policies have barely departed from uniform legal-tile sampling on the audited training states. RL601: 108913 states, mean normalized entropy 0.9999899058, mean total variation from uniform 0.0028800688. RL602: 108711 states, entropy 0.9999971594, TV 0.0015113087. CE601/602 entropies are 0.9999940629/0.9999991202. These are conditional probabilities at each tile decision; they do not assert that complete sampled trajectories are identical, or that unseen deployment states have exactly these entropies. They directly explain why a claim that the actors learned a strong selection preference is unsupported.

2. The feedback is coarse relative to the decision sequence. A 50-update branch contains about850 tile choices (seed601 mean850.8828, seed602 mean849.3047). All choices in a branch receive the same normalized branch advantage. Each actor receives only32 optimizer updates and128 branch returns. Initial output logits are all zero, and the loss includes an entropy bonus .01. Finite nonzero gradients and parameter updates establish execution, not successful policy learning. Sparse credit assignment, limited actor optimization and entropy regularization are plausible contributors; their individual causal effects have not been ablated. Averaging trajectory log-probabilities also reweights branches by their decision counts. Adam means one cannot infer the effective update merely by pointing to a 1/length factor.

3. Coverage information was supplied as features, not guaranteed as behavior. Each candidate gets scalar prediction statistics, three pseudo-class proportions, selected-class coverage and a novelty score. There are no region semantic embeddings, target-distribution prototypes, explicit coverage constraints, or a class-coverage reward term. Identical aggregate features can describe different semantic regions. The learned actors selected roughly90% pseudo-class0, similar to RANDOM; COVERAGE selected79.7151% class0,15.6135% class1,4.6714% class2. Confidence selected99.9972% class0. This is evidence about pseudo-label allocation, not pseudo-label correctness or a proof that class balance causes the observed performance differences.

4. The available actions only redistribute a mandatory budget. Eligible pixels are fixed by EMAconfidence>.7; the actor cannot recover useful pixels removed by this gate, correct the pseudo-target, skip an image, select fewer than floor(eligible/2), or disable the U update. Selected class weights are renormalized to the original eligible count; their average is therefore approximately2 over admitted selected pixels, while total U weight mass stays fixed. This isolates allocation but does not provide a way to reduce total harmful supervision. The half-budget/no-abstention recipe was our design decision, not a result established by the cited papers.

5. Seventeen of64 training groups had all four branch rewards negative. Group-relative normalization still gives a positive advantage to less harmful branches; this is not itself a policy-gradient bug. But without an abstention action, the best available choice can remain harmful. Example seed601/group3 rewards [-.00338812,-.00526091,-.00364199,-.00325065], advantages [.61664,-1.70561,.30185,.78711]. Positive advantages must not be interpreted as absolute improvement.

6. Improving relative to the existing update path and achieving positive continuation are different outcomes. In percentage points relative to ENTRY: OFF new−.8462/old−.6426; RANDOM new−.5734/old−.5715; pooledRL new−.5685/old−.5726; COVERAGE new−.5373/old−.5862. RL adds only+.00485pp new over RANDOM. Some U supervision mitigates the OFF degradation, but the learned policy adds almost nothing to random allocation and no tested policy reverses pooled degradation. OFF also degrades, so pseudo-label quality alone cannot explain everything.

7. A short training return has not translated into a positive final outcome. PooledRL new change is +.1468pp at MID150 but−.5685pp at FINAL300. The actor is trained on50-update branches and deployed continuously for200 updates. Training conditions are brightness.8/contrast1.2; development conditions are brightness1.2/contrast.8 with different image splits and streams. Training queries repeatedly use four authorized images for each role. Training reward positivity does not establish that rankings persist over longer horizons or shifted conditions. Dev0 improves both new/old; dev1–3 decline in both under pooledRL. These aggregate outcomes do not isolate which particular shift or optimizer mechanism causes degradation.

## What was transferred from papers, and what was not

- [Reinforced Co-Training](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1113/): performance-driven unlabeled-subset selection in a two-view co-training/Q-learning framework. We retained the idea of downstream-performance feedback but substituted fixed spatial tiles, a memory/EMA target, and a long sequence scored through REINFORCE. This is an inspired adaptation, not a reproduction.
- [Selective Pseudo-Labeling](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03438): considers correctness and representativeness; the state includes learned features and class summaries for selected/labeled and unlabeled pools, and its full method also uses target-margin learning. Our predicted class shares are a much narrower proxy for representativeness. We did not reproduce the complete state, objective or DQN.
- [SAMIX](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Hu_SAMIX_Reinforcing_SAM2_with_Semantic_Adapter_and_Reference_Selecting_Policy_CVPR_2026_paper.html): autoregressive complementary reference selection supplies dense context to a semantic-adapted SAM2 pseudo-label generator, with mask-quality-associated feedback. Our selector only masks an already-generated pseudo-target; it cannot improve that generator or retrieve new reference information. We borrowed conditional selection, not the full causal path from reference retrieval to better pseudo-labels.

Adding two mechanisms motivated by successful papers does not transfer their training signal, action effectiveness, model priors or operating conditions. The most concrete failure here is that the trained actors remain near uniform on observed training states; the second is that the available fixed-mass allocation mechanisms do not reverse the negative continuation path.

## Implications for the next decision, not an execution instruction

First require a train-only mechanism check showing that the policy actually changes allocation and that its choices rank above equal-budget simple controls. Use complete fixed diagnostics rather than choosing a checkpoint on Q_dev. Before another full deployment, align the unit receiving reward with the unit being selected; roughly850 decisions sharing one terminal signal is a weak starting point for a32-update pilot. An explicit, meaningful set of supervision choices with observable marginal benefits would be more diagnosable than merely increasing group size or stacking a further module.

Separately establish which update path permits positive new-task learning with acceptable old-task retention. OFF's negative continuation is a diagnostic warning, not evidence that freezing solves continual learning. A better teacher, different optimization, selective abstention, shorter reward horizon or stronger coverage control are hypotheses requiring separate authorization/protocols and controlled comparisons; none is a validated fix from these results.

Not established: hidden-U pseudo-label error rates, ground-truth class correctness of selected regions, supervised/U gradient conflict, the causal share of entropy/step count/features in near-uniformity, or independent generalization. No hidden labels or new Q_dev reads were used to fill these gaps.
